# Concept Evaluation

Use this rubric to select among creative directions and audit a finished plan or experience. Score decisions, not verbosity, polish, technical density, or confidence.

## Hard Gates

A candidate is ineligible when any gate fails:

- **Factual integrity**: Operational claims are supplied, verified, or explicitly provisional.
- **Utility floor**: Primary visitor tasks remain easy to find and complete.
- **Concept before technology**: The idea is meaningful without naming a framework, rendering technique, or effect.
- **Accessible equivalent**: The concept has a credible keyboard, touch, and reduced-motion expression.
- **Deliverable integrity**: The response stays within the requested stopping point; a plan-only response contains no technical blueprint.
- **Constraint integrity**: The finished work follows its Reference Receipts instead of contradicting them later.

Factual integrity applies at the point of use. An opening truth ledger does not rescue later invented customer-facing claims.

## Scored Dimensions

Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Domain truth** | Uses category stereotypes | Reflects recognizable subject matter | Reveals a specific human, material, or cultural truth |
| **Originality** | Repeats common web tropes | Contains some non-obvious choices | Produces a surprising direction that feels earned |
| **Ownability** | Could serve almost any subject | Includes several subject-specific choices | Replacing the subject would break the concept |
| **Coherence** | Sections and effects feel unrelated | Most decisions share a theme | Content, form, motion, and interaction obey one premise |
| **Interaction necessity** | Interaction is decorative | Interaction supports the theme | Interaction is how the visitor understands or participates in the idea |
| **Visitor intelligence** | Spectacle obstructs real tasks | Utility and expression coexist | Utility becomes a meaningful part of the experience |
| **Art-direction specificity** | Mood adjectives only | Defines usable visual ingredients | Establishes concrete rules for composition, type, imagery, material, and rhythm |
| **Restraint** | Accumulates fashionable techniques | Prioritizes a few devices | Clearly rejects distractions and concentrates impact |
| **Feasible ambition** | Ignores assets, devices, or cost | Has plausible fallbacks | Achieves a bold outcome through deliberate technical and production choices |
| **Recall** | Remembered as “a site with effects” | Has a recognizable visual hook | Leaves a concise idea or experience the visitor can retell |

Use the total to compare candidates, not to disguise judgment as mathematics. A lower-scoring candidate may still win when it uniquely satisfies a hard user constraint; record that reason.

Calibrate scores against the definitions. A 5 needs concrete exceptional evidence, and a perfect or near-perfect result should trigger another adversarial pass. The selected direction must retain at least one credible weakness, risk, or unresolved dependency.

## Stress Tests

### Noun-Substitution Test

Replace the subject with an unrelated business, product, or institution. If most of the plan still works, the direction is insufficiently ownable.

### Interaction-Removal Test

Remove the signature interaction. If meaning and visitor understanding remain unchanged, the interaction is decoration.

### First-Association Test

Ask whether the concept is merely the category's most obvious object enlarged, animated, or rendered in 3D. Literal concepts must demonstrate a second layer of meaning to survive.

### Trope-Inheritance Test

Check whether the plan reaches automatically for particles, crystalline fragments, dark voids, telemetry, dramatic metrics, destruction/reconstruction, protected reveals, custom cursors, or cinematic scroll. Retain any of these only when the subject and concept independently demand them.

### Ten-Second Utility Test

A visitor arriving from search, maps, or a shared link should locate the primary action or information without completing an expressive sequence.

### One-Sentence Recall Test

Describe what makes the experience memorable without listing visual effects or technologies. If that sentence is vague, return to concept selection.

### Fact-Trace Test

Inspect the proposed customer-facing experience rather than only its opening ledger. Every operational name, address, price, time, metric, service, amenity, policy, history, integration, and live capability must be supplied, verified, labelled provisional where used, or preserved as an unknown requirement. Any untraceable claim fails factual integrity.

### Deliverable-Boundary Test

Compare the final section with the request contract. In a concept or plan-only task, technical references, stacks, libraries, formulas, runtime budgets, API architecture, and implementation phases are evidence of scope drift, not completeness.

### Receipt-Contradiction Test

For each Reference Receipt constraint, find the corresponding decision in the final work and search for later contradictions. A correct paraphrase in the receipt does not offset a violation in the plan.

## Selection Record

For the selected direction, record:

1. Hard-gate result.
2. Dimension scores with evidence for every 1 or 5.
3. Strongest rejected alternative and why it lost.
4. Primary creative risk.
5. Fact, asset, or prototype needed to reduce that risk.
6. Fact-trace, deliverable-boundary, and receipt-contradiction results.

Keep this record concise. It is an explanation of the decision, not hidden reasoning or a performance of certainty.
